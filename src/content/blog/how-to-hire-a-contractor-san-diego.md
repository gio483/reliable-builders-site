Ask anyone who's been through a bad remodel and they'll tell you: the scariest part wasn't the money. It was realizing, halfway through, that they'd hired the wrong person.

The good news is that most bad experiences are avoidable. You just have to know what to ask — and what to watch out for.

## The red flag to run from

Let's start with the biggest one, because you may have already met it: **the high-pressure salesperson who won't leave your house until you sign.**

You know the type. They show up, quote a big scary number, then "call their manager" and magically drop the price if you commit tonight. Companies that sell this way — and there are several big ones in San Diego — care more about closing you than building for you.

A good contractor gives you the information and lets you decide. If someone is pressuring you to sign on the spot, that pressure is the whole strategy. Walk away.

## Seven questions to ask before you hire

1. **Are you licensed and insured?** Ask for the license number and proof of insurance. In California you can look up any contractor's license on the CSLB website. (Ours is CSLB #1139785.)
2. **Who will actually be doing the work?** Is it the person you're talking to, their own crew, or the cheapest subs they can find that week?
3. **Can I see and visit past projects?** Photos are good. Addresses you can drive by, or past clients you can call, are better.
4. **What's included — and what's not?** Get the scope in writing. Vague quotes are where "surprise" charges live.
5. **What's the timeline, honestly?** A real builder gives you a realistic schedule and explains what could change it.
6. **How will you keep me updated?** You should never have to wonder what's happening in your own home this week.
7. **What happens if something goes wrong?** How they answer this tells you everything about how they'll treat you when it counts.

## The green flags

The best contractors tend to share a few traits:

- **They educate you** instead of pressuring you.
- **They give you honest answers**, even when the honest answer is a smaller job for them.
- **They respect your budget** instead of quietly steering you past it.
- **Their work comes from referrals** — because people who trust them tell their friends.

## The bottom line

Hiring a contractor is really about hiring a person you'll be trusting inside your home for weeks or months. Trust your gut. If someone is honest, clear, and not pushing you, that's usually the one.
