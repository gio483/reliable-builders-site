Most of the stress people feel during a remodel comes down to one thing: the money didn't go the way they expected. The budget looked fine on paper, then reality showed up with a bigger bill.

The fix isn't a bigger wallet. It's a budget that tells you the truth from the start. Let's build one together.

## Start with a real number, not a hope

Before you fall in love with tile, you need a ballpark. Here's where honest San Diego pricing lands today:

- **Kitchen remodels:** about **$30K to $60K**, with most landing near the middle.
- **Bathroom remodels:** about **$25K to $50K**, depending on how much you're changing.
- **ADUs** (a separate small home on your lot): usually **starting around $150K**.

These are ranges, not promises. A kitchen where you keep the layout and swap finishes sits at the low end. Move walls, move plumbing, or add high-end everything, and you climb toward the top. The number depends on your choices — which is actually good news, because it means you have control.

## Know the three things that move the price

When you understand what drives cost, you stop guessing. Three things matter most:

1. **Are you moving plumbing or walls?** Keeping the sink and stove where they are saves real money. Moving them means new pipes and electrical, and that adds up fast.
2. **What finishes do you pick?** A countertop can cost $40 a square foot or $200 a square foot. Same for tile, cabinets, and fixtures. This is where budgets quietly balloon.
3. **How big is the space?** More square footage means more materials and more labor. Simple, but it matters.

## Build in a cushion — always

Here's the rule that saves people: **set aside 10% to 15% of your budget for surprises.**

Why? Because older homes hide things. You open a wall and find old wiring that isn't up to code, or a leak nobody knew about. A good contractor will tell you straight when this happens — but the money has to come from somewhere. If you planned for it, it's a small bump. If you didn't, it feels like a disaster.

So if your kitchen budget is $50K, treat it like $43K to $45K of "planned" work and keep the rest in reserve. If you don't need it, great — you came in under.

## Watch out for the quote that's too good

When you collect bids, you'll sometimes see one that's way lower than the rest. It's tempting. But a lowball quote usually means one of two things: the contractor left stuff out, or they plan to make it up later with "surprise" charges once you've already started.

This is where the honest-versus-pushy difference really shows. Some big remodel companies in San Diego quote a scary high number, then "call their manager" and drop the price if you sign tonight. That pressure is the whole game. A real builder gives you a clear, complete number and lets you think it over.

Ask every contractor the same question: **"What's included, and what's not?"** Get it in writing. Vague quotes are where the pain lives.

## Don't forget the costs outside the contract

A few expenses live outside the build itself. Plan for them so they don't catch you:

- **Permits**, if your project needs them. Fees vary by city, so ask early.
- **Eating out or a temporary setup** if your kitchen is down for a few weeks.
- **Small design choices** — hardware, paint, light fixtures — that add up in the final stretch.

None of these are huge on their own, but together they're real. A quick list up front keeps them from feeling like a pile-on at the end.

## Match the project to your goal

One last thing: spend where it matters to *you*. If you love to cook and host, your [kitchen](/services/kitchens) is worth the investment. If a growing family or an aging parent is the reason, an [ADU](/services/adus) might be the smarter dollar. There's no single right answer — only the one that fits your life.

A good contractor will actually talk you *out* of spending on things that won't pay off for you. That's the kind of conversation worth having before a single dollar is spent.

## The bottom line

A good remodel budget isn't about spending less. It's about knowing — before you start — what things really cost, where money hides, and how much cushion to keep. Do that, and the whole project feels calm instead of scary.
