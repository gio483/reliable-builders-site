If your kitchen is a long, narrow room with counters running down two walls (or one wall and a run across from it), you've got a galley kitchen. It's one of the most common layouts in older San Diego homes — and honestly, one of the most misunderstood.

People assume a galley is a problem. It isn't. Cooks actually love galleys, because everything is within arm's reach. The real job in a galley remodel isn't to fight the shape — it's to make that shape work beautifully. Here's how.

## Why the galley layout is secretly great

Before you tear it apart, know what you've got. In a galley kitchen:

- **Everything is close.** The sink, stove, and fridge are just a step or two apart. You're not walking laps to cook dinner.
- **There's counter on both sides.** Prep on one side, cook on the other. Pro kitchens are built this way on purpose.
- **It's efficient to remodel.** Two straight runs of cabinet and counter are simpler — and often cheaper — than a complicated layout with islands and angles.

So the goal is to keep that efficiency while fixing the two things galleys usually get wrong: feeling cramped, and feeling dark.

## Make it feel wider than it is

A galley can feel tight because your eye hits walls and cabinets on both sides. A few honest moves open it right up:

- **Light colors on both walls and cabinets.** In a narrow room, dark colors close in on you. Light finishes push the walls back.
- **Skip uppers on one side.** Open shelves or even a bare wall on one run makes the whole room breathe. You lose a little storage and gain a lot of openness.
- **One continuous countertop color.** A single, calm counter surface running the length of the room makes it feel longer and less busy.
- **Flooring that runs the long way.** Boards or tile that run down the length of the galley stretch the room out visually.

## Fix the lighting — it's the biggest difference

Galley kitchens are often the darkest room in the house, stuck in the middle with no windows. Lighting is where you get the most bang for your buck:

1. **Bright, even overhead light** so there are no dark zones.
2. **Under-cabinet lights** on your main prep side, so you're not chopping in your own shadow.
3. **A light, reflective backsplash** to bounce what light you have around the room.

Clients are always surprised how much bigger and nicer a galley feels once it's actually well-lit. It's often the cheapest upgrade with the biggest payoff.

## Keep the walkway clear

This is the one rule you can't break in a galley: **leave enough room to walk and work.** A couple of honest guidelines:

- You want a comfortable gap down the middle so you can open the oven or dishwasher without blocking the whole path.
- If two people cook in your house, a little extra width in that walkway is worth protecting — don't let deep cabinets eat it up.

Sometimes clients ask about opening one end of the galley into the next room to get more light and flow. Occasionally that's a great move. Other times the wall is load-bearing or full of plumbing and the payoff is small. We'll tell you honestly which one your house is before you spend a dime on it.

## Smart storage for a narrow kitchen

Since floor space is tight, go up and go clever:

- **Tall cabinets** that reach higher use the vertical space you're not using now.
- **Deep drawers** instead of low cabinets — easier to reach, and they hold more.
- **Pull-outs and dividers** so the back of every cabinet is actually usable.

## What it costs

Here's some good news: because a galley is two straight runs, it's often one of the more budget-friendly kitchens to remodel well. It still lives inside the typical $30K–$60K range, but the simple shape tends to land it on the lower end — fewer odd corners, no island, less custom cabinetry. That's a real advantage of the layout you already have.
