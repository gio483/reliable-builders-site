You've probably heard the term "ADU" by now. But there's a smaller cousin that fewer people know about: the **Junior ADU**, or **JADU**. If a full ADU feels like more than you need — or more than your budget allows — a JADU might be the smart middle path.

Let's break it down in plain English.

## What is a JADU?

A Junior ADU is a small living unit built **inside the walls of your existing home**. Usually it's created out of a bedroom or another room you already have.

The key differences from a regular ADU:

- **It's small.** A JADU is capped at a modest size — think one room plus a small kitchen area, not a full separate home.
- **It's inside your house.** It's carved out of your existing home's footprint, not built as a new structure in the yard.
- **It has its own entrance.** A JADU needs a separate way in and out.
- **It needs a small kitchen.** An "efficiency kitchen" — compact, but real.
- **The bathroom can be shared or private.** A JADU is allowed to share a bathroom with the main house, which is one reason it can cost less.

Think of it as a studio apartment tucked inside your home.

## JADU vs. regular ADU: the simple version

Here's the easiest way to keep them straight:

- A **detached ADU** is a brand-new, standalone home in your backyard. Its own everything. These generally **start around $150,000**.
- A **garage conversion** turns your existing garage into a living space — usually **less expensive**, since the shell is already there.
- A **JADU** is a small unit built inside your current home. It's typically the **most budget-friendly** path, because you're reusing space and systems you already have.

None of these is "better." They solve different problems at different price points.

## When a JADU makes sense

A Junior ADU can be the right call when:

- **Budget is tight.** It's often the lowest-cost way to add a legal unit.
- **You have a spare room.** If a bedroom is sitting unused, converting it may be simpler than building new.
- **You want to keep it simple.** No new foundation, no new roof, no running utilities across the yard.
- **You need space for one person.** A JADU is perfect for a single family member, a caregiver, or a modest rental.

Because it shares some systems with your main house and lives inside the existing footprint, a JADU usually means less construction — and less construction usually means less money and less time.

## The trade-offs to know

A JADU is smaller and more connected to your main home than a standalone ADU. That's the whole point, but it's worth being clear-eyed about:

- **Less privacy.** It's attached to your home, sometimes sharing a bathroom.
- **Less space.** It's a compact unit, not a full house.
- **Less rental income than a full ADU.** Smaller unit, smaller rent.

If you want a true separate home with full privacy and the most long-term value, a detached ADU is the better fit. If you want the affordable, simple option and the space works, a JADU is hard to beat.

## Build it legal — this still applies

A JADU may be smaller and simpler, but it's still a real living unit, and it still has to be **permitted and done to code**. That means a proper, if compact, kitchen, safe electrical and plumbing, and city sign-off.

The rule we live by never changes: a permitted unit is an asset that adds value. An unpermitted one is a liability that costs you at resale, at refinance, or with the city. We only build them the right way.

And here's the encouraging part — California's statewide ADU laws made adding JADUs much easier than it used to be. For a lot of San Diego homeowners, it's more doable than they expect.

A Junior ADU is a great fit when you want to add legal, livable space without the cost and scope of a full build. It's the quiet, practical option a lot of homeowners don't know they have.
