"What's this going to cost me?" It's a fair question, and most contractors dodge it. We won't.

The honest answer: **most bathroom remodels in San Diego land between $25,000 and $50,000.** A straightforward guest bath sits near the bottom. A large primary bath with custom tile, a big walk-in shower, and glass sits near the top. Where your project falls depends on a handful of choices we'll walk through below.

## What you're actually paying for

A bathroom is a small room with a lot packed into it. Your money is split across a few big buckets:

- **Labor and skilled trades** — demo, plumbing, electrical, tile, and finish work. This is usually the biggest slice, and it's where craftsmanship lives.
- **Materials and fixtures** — tile, vanity, countertop, shower glass, faucets, and lighting. You control a lot of this with your selections.
- **The stuff behind the walls** — waterproofing, backing, and framing. You never see it, but it's what makes the rest last.
- **Permits and prep** — not every bath needs a permit, but moving plumbing or electrical often does.

## What pushes the price up

A few choices move the number the most:

- **Moving plumbing.** Turning a tub into a walk-in shower, or relocating the toilet, means opening walls and floors. It adds cost, but it's often worth it.
- **Custom tile and glass.** A full tile shower with a frameless glass door costs more than a simple tub surround — and looks like a different room.
- **Higher-end fixtures.** A $300 faucet and a $1,200 faucet both turn on. The difference is feel and finish, and that's your call.
- **Older homes.** Once the walls are open, we sometimes find old plumbing or water damage that needs fixing. A good contractor budgets a cushion for this instead of surprising you.

## What keeps the price down

You have more control than you'd think:

- **Keep the layout.** If the toilet, sink, and shower stay where they are, you save real money.
- **Pick smart materials.** There are beautiful tiles at every price. We'll show you where to spend and where to save.
- **Buy once.** Changing your mind mid-project is the most expensive thing you can do. Deciding on your selections early keeps the budget steady.

## The part of the price nobody shows you

Here's where cheap bids get dangerous. **The waterproofing behind your tile is where the corner gets cut.**

A bathroom is the wettest room in your home. Behind every good shower is a waterproof membrane and a floor that slopes correctly toward the drain. You can't see either one once the tile goes on. A bargain crew can skip the membrane or rush the slope, hand you a shower that looks perfect, and be long gone by the time water finds its way into the wall. The first sign is usually a stain on the ceiling below — and by then the fix costs far more than the savings ever did.

This is exactly why we do our own tile and waterproofing in-house instead of handing it to the lowest sub. Spending an extra day on the layers you'll never see is the difference between a bathroom that lasts 25 years and one you're redoing in five. When you compare bids, the cheapest one is often cheap because of what it leaves out here.

## How to compare bids honestly

If you get three quotes and one is thousands less, don't assume you found a deal. Ask each contractor:

- Are you licensed and insured? (Ours is CSLB License #1139785.)
- Who does the tile and waterproofing — your own crew or the cheapest sub?
- What happens if you open the wall and find a problem?
- Can I see photos of your finished work?

The answers tell you more than the price does. A real contractor will give you a clear number up front, explain what's in it, and keep you posted so you're never guessing.
