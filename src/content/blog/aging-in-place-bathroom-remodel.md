When people hear "aging-in-place bathroom," they picture a hospital — cold metal bars, a plastic seat, that clinical feel. It doesn't have to be that way. A well-planned bathroom can be safer for the years ahead and still look like a beautiful, modern room. Whether you're planning for yourself, a parent, or a home you want to stay in for good, here's how to do it right.

## Who this is really for

Aging-in-place isn't only for people who are already older. We build these bathrooms for:

- **Homeowners planning ahead** who want to stay in the house they love for decades.
- **Families building an ADU for an aging parent** — a safe, private bathroom is often the whole point.
- **Anyone recovering from an injury** who suddenly understands why a step-free shower matters.

The goal is simple: a bathroom that's easy and safe to use now, and still works just as well twenty years from now, without a second remodel.

## The features that matter most

A few smart choices do most of the work. None of them have to look medical:

- **A curbless (zero-threshold) shower.** No lip to step over means no tripping and easy entry — even with a walker or wheelchair someday. It also happens to look sleek and modern.
- **A built-in bench or seat.** A tiled bench built into the shower looks custom, not clinical, and it's there when you need to sit.
- **Grab bars that look like towel bars.** Today's grab bars come in finishes that match your fixtures. Done right, guests won't even know they're grab bars.
- **A handheld shower on a slide bar.** Easy to use sitting or standing, at any height.
- **Slip-resistant floor tile.** Smaller floor tiles with more grout lines actually give better grip underfoot — safety and style in one choice.
- **A comfort-height toilet and a wider doorway.** Small changes that make a big difference in daily comfort and access.

## Plan the walls now, even if you wait on the bars

Here's a tip that saves money later: even if you're not ready for grab bars today, have your contractor **add solid backing (called blocking) inside the walls** during the remodel. It's cheap to do while the walls are open, and it means grab bars can be installed anywhere, securely, whenever you want them — no tearing tile back off. Planning ahead is almost free. Retrofitting is not.

## What it costs

An aging-in-place bathroom usually falls in the same range as any quality remodel — **most land between $25,000 and $50,000** in San Diego. A curbless shower can add a little, because the floor has to be built up correctly to create the slope without a curb. But the features that make a bathroom safer, like blocking in the walls and slip-resistant tile, cost very little when they're built in from the start. It's far cheaper to do it once, now, than to remodel twice.

## The hidden step that keeps it safe

Here's what gets overlooked: **a curbless shower only works if the waterproofing and slope are done exactly right.**

Think about it. A normal shower has a curb — a little wall — to keep water in. A curbless shower doesn't. The only thing keeping water from running across your bathroom floor is a correctly built slope toward the drain and a waterproof membrane under the tile. Get that slope wrong and you've got water on the floor, which is a slipping hazard — the exact opposite of what you were going for. Skip the membrane and that water soaks straight into the wall and framing.

This is exactly why we do our own tile and waterproofing in-house instead of handing it to the cheapest sub. For an aging-in-place bathroom, the invisible layers aren't just about lasting 25 years — they're about safety from day one. It takes real skill to build a curbless shower that drains perfectly and stays dry. That's the part you want done by hand, carefully, by people who own the result.

## The bottom line

A bathroom built for the years ahead can be safe *and* beautiful — there's no trade-off when it's planned well. The trick is thinking ahead: the right features, solid backing in the walls, and waterproofing done by people who take it seriously. Do it once, do it right, and enjoy your home for a long time.
