Grantville is one of the oldest corners of San Diego — the valley floor near the original Mission, just east of Mission Valley, where the trolley's Green Line runs and the neighborhood has spent the last two decades steadily reinventing itself. It's a mix: long-held older homes on quiet residential streets, newer infill, and pockets of ongoing redevelopment around the Grantville trolley station. For homeowners here, that combination is the appeal — you get central location, transit access, and a real neighborhood, often in a house with some age and character to it. When those homeowners call us, it's usually to bring an older kitchen, bathroom, or the whole property into the present without losing what made it worth buying.

Grantville rewards a builder who understands older homes and the City of San Diego's process — because here you frequently get both at once.

## The homes here

Grantville traces back further than almost anywhere else in the city — this stretch of the San Diego River valley sits beside the site of California's first mission, and the area was on the map long before the post-war building booms. The residential housing stock that most homeowners actually live in is a patchwork: older homes of varying eras on the established streets, mixed with newer construction and multi-family infill that's gone up as the neighborhood has redeveloped around the trolley line over the last twenty-plus years.

For a remodel, that range is the defining feature. On the older homes, we see what age brings — galvanized or early copper supply lines, cast-iron drains, ungrounded wiring, and electrical panels sized for a much smaller load — and we budget for it honestly instead of pretending a 60- or 70-year-old house is going to behave like a new one. On the newer infill, the systems are modern but the finishes are often builder-grade and ripe for an upgrade. Either way, the first thing we do is figure out exactly what we're working with, so the estimate reflects the real house rather than a hopeful guess.

Grantville's central position is a genuine advantage once a home is updated. A smart remodel here gives you a modern kitchen or bath in a location that's hard to beat — minutes from Mission Valley, the 8, and the trolley.

## Permitting in Grantville

Grantville is part of the City of San Diego, so permits for remodeling work here run through the **City of San Diego's Development Services Department**. A kitchen or bathroom remodel that stays within the existing footprint — cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures, lighting, and like-for-like plumbing and electrical — is a permittable project, and we pull the permits and schedule the inspections so you're never the one working through the City's system.

Anything structural or expanding — removing a wall to open a kitchen (we confirm whether it's load-bearing and engineer a beam if needed), relocating plumbing, upgrading a panel, or adding square footage — steps up the review. Grantville also sits inside an area the City has long targeted for planned growth and transit-oriented development, so zoning and overlay details can come into play on bigger projects in a way they wouldn't on a sleepy cul-de-sac elsewhere. We check which rules apply to your specific parcel before we quote, so the permit path is built into the plan from day one rather than a surprise that stalls the job partway through.

## ADUs in Grantville

ADUs — accessory dwelling units, or granny flats — are especially relevant in Grantville, and not only for the usual reasons (housing an aging parent or adult kid, or earning rental income). Because the neighborhood is served by the trolley, it falls within the kind of transit-oriented area where the City of San Diego's ADU incentives can be most generous. California's statewide ADU laws already made these far easier to build than a decade ago, and the City layers its own program on top of that framework — the local rules live in San Diego Municipal Code Chapter 14 — including a streamlined review track and, in transit-served areas, additional flexibility on the number of units a lot may support.

What that actually means for your property depends entirely on your parcel — its zoning, lot size, setbacks, access, and utilities — and on how close you are to the transit line. That's exactly why the first thing we do is a feasibility check, before anyone gets attached to a floor plan, so you know what's genuinely possible rather than what's possible in theory. From there we handle the permit set, the engineering, and the build, so the finished ADU is legal, financeable, and somewhere you'd actually want to live or rent. Most ADU projects start around **$150K** and rise with size, site conditions, and finishes.
