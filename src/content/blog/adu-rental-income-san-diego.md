Lots of San Diego homeowners ask the same hopeful question: "If I build an ADU, can it pay for itself?"

The honest answer is: often, yes — over time. But it depends on a few things, and we'd rather give you the real picture than a hyped-up promise. So let's talk about how the money actually works.

## How an ADU makes money

An ADU is a second home on your property. Once it's built, you can rent it out and collect monthly rent, the same way any rental works.

That rent does two useful things:

- **It brings in income** every month.
- **It helps cover the cost** of building the unit in the first place.

Over enough months and years, the rent can add up to more than you spent to build. That's what people mean when they say an ADU "pays for itself." It's not instant — it's a long game. But it's a real one.

## The honest cost side

Before we talk about income, let's be straight about what it costs to get there, because that's half the math.

- A **detached ADU** — a new standalone unit — generally **starts around $150,000** and goes up with size and finish.
- A **garage conversion** usually **costs less**, since the foundation and shell already exist.
- A **Junior ADU (JADU)**, built inside your existing home, is often the most budget-friendly path of all.

A lower build cost means a shorter path to "paying for itself." That's one reason we always look at which type truly fits your lot and your goals — the cheapest unit that meets your needs often makes the best financial sense, and we'll tell you that honestly even when a bigger build would earn us more.

## Why rent isn't the only payoff

Monthly rent is the obvious benefit, but it's not the whole story. A well-built ADU also:

- **Adds value to your property.** A legal second unit makes your home worth more, whether or not you ever sell.
- **Gives you flexibility.** Rent it today, move a family member in later, or use it yourself down the road.
- **Creates options.** Income now, housing for aging parents or adult kids later, a home office after that.

So even if the rent alone takes years to cover the build, you own an asset that's working for you the entire time.

## The one rule that makes or breaks the money: build it legal

This is the part we can't say loudly enough. For an ADU to be a true income asset, it has to be **permitted, engineered, and inspected**.

Here's why it matters so much for the money:

- **A legal ADU can be rented with confidence** and counts as real, recognized square footage.
- **It adds value at resale and helps at refinance**, because banks and buyers recognize it.
- **An unpermitted unit does the opposite.** It can scare off buyers, create problems with the city, and turn into a cost instead of an income source.

Put simply: a permitted ADU is an asset. An unpermitted one is a liability. The whole "pays for itself" idea only works when it's done by the book. We only build them the right way — no shortcuts.

## California made this easier

There's real good news here. Over the last few years, California's statewide ADU laws made it much easier and more predictable to add a unit. More San Diego lots qualify than most homeowners think, which means more people can turn their backyard into income-producing space.

## What a real income-ready ADU includes

To be a unit a tenant actually wants to rent — and the city actually approves — your ADU should include:

- **Feasibility and planning** — checking setbacks, utilities, and what your lot allows before you spend on drawings.
- **Permits and engineering** — so it's legal, safe, and financeable.
- **Full utilities** — electrical, plumbing, and heating/cooling done to code.
- **A real finished interior** — a proper kitchen, a full bath, and durable finishes that make it feel like home.

## The honest takeaway

Can an ADU pay for itself in San Diego? Often, yes — if you build the right type for your lot, keep the budget sensible, and do it legal so it holds its value. It's a long-term asset, not a get-rich-quick move, and that's exactly why it's a smart one.
