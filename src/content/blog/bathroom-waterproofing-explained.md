Most of what you pay for in a bathroom, you can see — the tile, the vanity, the glass. But the single most important part of the job is the part you'll never see again once it's done: the waterproofing. Get it right and your bathroom lasts decades. Get it wrong and the prettiest tile in the world is just a lid on a slow problem.

Here's how it actually works, in plain English, so you can make sure yours is done right.

## Why a bathroom needs waterproofing at all

A bathroom is the wettest room in your house. Every shower sends gallons of water at the walls and floor. Here's the part that surprises people: **tile and grout are not waterproof.** Water passes right through grout lines over time. That's fine — as long as there's a layer behind the tile to catch that water and send it back to the drain.

That hidden layer is the waterproofing. Without it, water soaks into the wall board, the wood framing, and eventually the room below. Wet wood rots. Rot brings mold. And none of it shows up until the damage is already expensive.

## The two things that have to be right

Good waterproofing comes down to two things working together:

- **A waterproof membrane.** This is a layer — a sheet or a painted-on coating — that goes behind the tile on the shower walls and floor. It's the real barrier. The tile is just the pretty, durable surface on top of it.
- **The right slope.** The shower floor has to tilt gently toward the drain so water actually runs out instead of sitting in corners. The industry standard is about a quarter-inch of fall for every foot. Too little and water pools. Too much and you feel it under your feet.

When both are done correctly, water does exactly what you want: it hits the tile, works its way to the membrane, and drains away. When either one is skipped or rushed, water goes where it shouldn't.

## Where corners get cut

This is the step cheap bids quietly skip, because you can't see it and it costs an extra day. A bargain crew can:

- Skip the membrane and rely on the tile and grout alone (they'll fail).
- Rush the slope so water pools in the corners of the shower floor.
- Waterproof the walls but not the floor, or the other way around.
- Use the wrong products, or not seal the corners and the drain connection.

The bathroom will look perfect on the final day. The problem shows up months or years later — a musty smell, a loose tile, a stain on the ceiling of the room below. By then the fix means tearing the shower back out, and it costs far more than doing it right the first time ever would have.

## How to make sure yours is done right

You don't need to be a builder to protect yourself. Just ask a few questions before you hire anyone:

- **Who does your waterproofing — your own crew or a sub?** You want a contractor who owns this step, not one who farms it out to the lowest bidder.
- **What membrane system do you use?** A real answer (a named product and method) beats a vague one.
- **Can I see the shower before the tile goes on?** A good contractor is happy to show you the membrane and the slope during the build. That's the moment to look.
- **Are you licensed and insured?** (Ours is CSLB License #1139785.)

If a contractor gets cagey about any of this, that tells you something.

## The bottom line

Waterproofing isn't the exciting part of a bathroom remodel. Nobody shows it off. But it's the part that decides whether your investment holds up — and it's the first thing a cheap bid leaves out. Pay attention to the layers you'll never see, and everything you can see will last.
