## The homes here

La Mesa incorporated in 1912, and a lot of its charm comes from how much of its early character survived. You'll find 1920s Craftsman and Spanish bungalows near the Village and the college, big waves of post-war and mid-century homes from the 1940s through the 1960s across the flatlands, and custom hillside houses stepping up toward Mount Helix and Fletcher Hills. It's one of the most architecturally varied small cities in the county.

For a remodel, that variety means one thing above all: **expect surprises behind the walls.** Homes built across six or seven decades were wired, plumbed, and framed to the codes of their day. We routinely open up La Mesa kitchens and baths to find old galvanized supply lines, cloth-wrapped wiring, two-prong circuits with no ground, cast-iron drains, and the occasional layer of someone's 1980s "upgrade" done without a permit. None of that is a reason to panic — it's just the reality of remodeling a home with history, and it's exactly why we give you an honest number up front instead of a lowball that balloons the moment we find the real condition of things.

The upside is that these homes have bones, light, and proportion that builders stopped giving tract houses decades ago. A La Mesa kitchen remodel done right doesn't fight the house — it brings the systems and finishes up to today while keeping what made someone fall in love with the place to begin with.

## Permitting in La Mesa

Permits for remodeling work in La Mesa run through the **City of La Mesa's Community Development Department**, which handles building, planning, and engineering. A straightforward kitchen or bathroom remodel that stays within the existing footprint — new cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures, lighting, and like-for-like plumbing and electrical — is a permittable project, and we pull the permits and schedule the inspections so you're never the one standing in line at City Hall.

Where it gets more involved is anything structural or anything that expands the home: removing a wall to open up a kitchen (we confirm whether it's load-bearing and engineer a beam if it is), moving plumbing, adding square footage, or building an ADU. Hillside lots near Mount Helix can also trigger grading or slope considerations the flatland lots don't. We sort out which track a project falls on before we quote it, so the permit path is part of the plan from day one — not a surprise that stalls the job halfway through.

## ADUs in La Mesa

ADUs — accessory dwelling units, or granny flats — are one of the most common reasons La Mesa homeowners call us, usually for aging parents, adult kids, or long-term rental income. California's statewide ADU laws have made these far easier to build than they were a decade ago, and La Mesa follows that framework alongside its own local zoning.

What that means in practice: on most single-family lots you can build a detached ADU or convert an existing garage, within the state's size and setback rules. The details — exactly how big, how close to the property line, whether your lot and utilities support it — depend on your specific parcel, and that's the first thing we check before anyone falls in love with a floor plan. La Mesa's older neighborhoods often have detached garages that convert well, while hillside lots sometimes favor a purpose-built detached unit that works with the slope. We handle the feasibility check, the permit set, the engineering, and the build, so the finished ADU is legal, financeable, and something you'd actually want to live in.
