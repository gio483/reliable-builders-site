The best way to pick the right contractor isn't to listen to their pitch. It's to ask good questions and watch how they answer. An honest builder welcomes these. A pushy one gets uncomfortable. That difference tells you almost everything.

Here are ten questions to ask before you hire — and what a good answer sounds like.

## 1. Are you licensed and insured?

This is the first filter. In California, you can look up any contractor's license on the CSLB website. A real builder hands you the number without flinching. (Ours is CSLB #1139785.) Insurance matters too — it protects you if someone gets hurt or something gets damaged on your job.

*Good answer:* a license number and proof of insurance, offered freely.

## 2. Who will actually be doing the work?

Some companies sell you with a smooth salesperson, then hand the job to whichever crew is cheapest that week. Ask who's really on site, and who's in charge of it day to day.

*Good answer:* a clear chain — their own crew or trusted, long-time subs, with one person responsible.

## 3. Can I see your past work — and talk to past clients?

Photos are nice. Addresses you can drive by, or past customers you can actually call, are better. Real builders are proud to connect you.

*Good answer:* "Here are a few projects, and here are two clients who said you could call them."

## 4. What's included in the price — and what's not?

This one question prevents most "surprise" charges. Vague quotes are where hidden costs hide. You want the scope in writing, line by line.

*Good answer:* a detailed, written scope that says what's covered and what isn't.

## 5. What's the timeline, honestly?

Notice the word *honestly*. A good contractor gives you a realistic schedule and explains what could stretch it — a permit delay, a backordered material, a surprise behind a wall.

*Good answer:* a real range with the honest reasons it might change.

## 6. What happens if you find a problem mid-project?

Older homes hide things. The question is how they'll handle it. Will they stop, show you, and explain the cost? Or quietly add charges and hope you don't notice?

*Good answer:* "We stop, show you exactly what we found, and get your okay before spending a dollar more."

## 7. How will you keep me updated?

You should never have to wonder what's happening in your own home this week. Ask how and how often they'll communicate.

*Good answer:* a clear rhythm — a weekly check-in, a point person, a way to reach them.

## 8. Do you handle permits and inspections?

A licensed builder treats permits as part of the job. In San Diego, the rules change depending on whether you're in the city, El Cajon, La Mesa, Santee, or the unincorporated county — so you want someone who knows your area.

*Good answer:* "Yes, we pull the permits and schedule the inspections for you."

## 9. How do you handle changes once we've started?

Plans shift. Maybe you decide on a different tile, or want to add something. Ask how changes get priced and approved so nothing happens behind your back.

*Good answer:* a written change-order process you sign off on before work proceeds.

## 10. What happens if something goes wrong after the job's done?

How a contractor answers this tells you how they'll treat you when it counts. Ask about their warranty and what they do if an issue shows up later.

*Good answer:* a clear warranty and a genuine "we'll make it right."

## The one red flag that beats all the answers

Watch *how* they respond as much as *what* they say. The biggest warning sign in this whole industry is **the high-pressure salesperson who won't leave until you sign.** They quote a big scary number, "call their manager," and magically drop the price if you commit tonight. Several large San Diego remodel companies sell exactly this way.

A good contractor gives you the information and lets you decide on your own time. If someone is pushing you to sign on the spot, that pressure *is* the strategy. Walk away.

## The bottom line

You're not just hiring a company. You're choosing a person you'll trust inside your home for weeks or months. These ten questions, and an honest, unhurried set of answers, will point you to the right one.
