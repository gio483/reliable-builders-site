It's one of the most common questions we hear: "Should I keep the tub, or go with a big walk-in shower?" There's no single right answer — it depends on your home, your family, and how you actually live. Here's the honest breakdown so you can decide for yourself.

## The case for a walk-in shower

Walk-in showers are what most people want today, and for good reason:

- **They feel bigger and more modern.** A frameless glass shower opens up a small bathroom and makes the whole room look custom.
- **They're easier to clean.** No tub ledge to scrub, no sliding doors with grimy tracks.
- **They're easier to step into.** A low or zero-threshold entry is kinder to your knees now and much safer as you get older.
- **They're great for resale** — in a primary bathroom, buyers expect a nice shower more than a tub.

The trade-off: a walk-in shower usually costs a bit more than a basic tub, especially with custom tile and glass. And if it's your only bathroom, losing the tub can hurt you at resale.

## The case for keeping a tub

Tubs aren't going anywhere, and in the right spot they're the smart choice:

- **Families with young kids** almost always want at least one tub. Bathing a toddler in a shower is no fun.
- **Resale math.** Most buyers want at least one tub in the house. If you only have one bathroom, keep a tub in it.
- **You actually take baths.** If a long soak is how you unwind, don't let a trend talk you out of it.

A simple rule we give clients: **if this is your only full bathroom, keep a tub somewhere in the house.** If it's a primary bath and there's a tub elsewhere, a walk-in shower is usually the better call.

## What about cost?

Here's where honesty matters. A like-for-like tub swap is often the cheaper project. But the moment you convert a tub into a walk-in shower, you're usually moving plumbing and opening up the floor — and that adds cost. It's often worth it, but you deserve to know up front, not halfway through.

Across our bathroom projects, most land in the **$25,000 to $50,000** range, and the shower-vs-tub choice is one of the bigger levers inside that number. We'll show you exactly how your pick changes the price before you commit to anything.

## The thing that matters more than either

Here's what most people never think about: **whether you choose a shower or a tub, what makes it last is the waterproofing underneath.**

A walk-in shower is the wettest spot in your home. Behind that beautiful tile there has to be a waterproof membrane, and the shower floor has to slope correctly toward the drain — enough that water runs out, not so much that you feel it underfoot. You can't see either one once the tile is on. Skip the membrane or rush the slope to save a day, and the shower can look flawless while water quietly works its way into the wall and the framing. The first sign is usually a musty smell or a stain on the ceiling below.

This is exactly why we do our own tile and waterproofing in-house instead of handing it to the cheapest sub. A gorgeous shower built on a bad foundation is just an expensive problem waiting to happen. Done right, it lasts decades.

## How to decide

Ask yourself three quick questions:

1. **Is this my only full bathroom?** If yes, keep a tub.
2. **Do I have young kids, or plan to?** If yes, keep at least one tub.
3. **Is this a primary bath with a tub elsewhere?** Then a walk-in shower will probably make you happiest.

There's no wrong answer when it fits your life. The wrong move is letting a pushy salesperson decide for you, or paying for a conversion you didn't need.

## The bottom line

Walk-in shower or tub, the right choice is the one that fits how you actually live — and the right contractor is the one who helps you think it through instead of upselling you. Whichever you pick, it's only as good as what's built behind the wall.
