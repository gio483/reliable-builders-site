Casa de Oro sits just east of La Mesa, wrapped around the base and slopes of Mount Helix. If you live here, you already know the mix: comfortable mid-century homes along Campo Road and the flatter streets, then custom hillside houses climbing the grade toward the Helix cross. We remodel kitchens, bathrooms, and ADUs across all of it — and since our shop is only a few minutes west in La Mesa, Casa de Oro is genuinely part of our backyard, not a job we drive across the county for.

The detail worth knowing up front: Casa de Oro is **unincorporated**. It's part of the Casa de Oro-Mount Helix community, which falls under the County of San Diego rather than any city. That shapes how a remodel gets permitted, and it's one of the first things we nail down before giving you a number.

## The homes here

Casa de Oro's housing leans heavily toward the 1950s through the 1970s — a real stretch of East County mid-century building — with custom homes of all eras stepping up the slopes of Mount Helix above it. Down along Campo Road you'll find a denser mix; up the hill, it's lower-density, higher-end custom homes with the views that make Mount Helix what it is. It's a community where a modest post-war house and a sprawling hillside custom can sit a few streets apart.

For a remodel, that era mix means one honest expectation: **the house will show its real condition once we open it up.** A 1950s or 1960s Casa de Oro kitchen or bathroom was wired, plumbed, and framed to the codes of its day. We routinely find aging galvanized supply lines, two-prong circuits with no ground, original cast-iron drains, and the occasional unpermitted update a prior owner added without inspection. East County builders of that era also treated air conditioning as optional, so HVAC and insulation are often overdue for attention too. None of that is cause for alarm — it's simply what remodeling a home with history looks like, and it's exactly why we quote an honest number that accounts for it rather than a lowball that climbs the first week.

The reward is that these homes have proportion, light, and lots that newer construction rarely matches — and on the hillside, views worth designing around. A Casa de Oro remodel done right brings the systems and finishes up to today without erasing what made the place worth buying. That balance — modernizing what needs it, keeping what's worth keeping — is a lot of what we do here, and it's a conversation we're happy to have before any demo starts.

## Permitting in Casa de Oro

Because Casa de Oro is unincorporated, permits run through the **County of San Diego's Planning & Development Services** — the Building Services Division that handles residential work in the unincorporated areas. That's a different office and process than a neighboring La Mesa homeowner would use, and we handle the whole thing: application, plan set, and inspections. You never deal with the County counter yourself.

A kitchen or bathroom remodel that stays inside the existing footprint — new cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures, lighting, and like-for-like plumbing and electrical — is the simpler, more straightforward track. It gets more involved with anything structural or anything that expands the home: removing a wall to open a kitchen (we confirm whether it's load-bearing and engineer a beam if it is), moving plumbing, adding square footage, or building an ADU. Casa de Oro's Mount Helix hillside lots can also trigger grading or slope considerations that the flatter streets don't. We determine which track your Casa de Oro project falls on before we quote it, so the County permit path is part of the plan from the start — not a mid-job surprise.

## ADUs in Casa de Oro

ADUs are one of the most common reasons Casa de Oro homeowners reach out, usually for an aging parent, an adult kid priced out of the rental market, or long-term rental income. The area's mix of flatter lots near Campo Road and larger hillside parcels gives real range in what's possible.

Because Casa de Oro is unincorporated, your ADU follows **California's statewide ADU laws as administered by the County of San Diego**. That framework now lets most single-family lots add a detached ADU, convert an existing garage, or add a junior ADU, within the state's size and setback rules — and recent state updates have made it easier than it was years ago. On the hillside, slope and access often shape the design as much as the rules do; a purpose-built detached unit sometimes works better with the grade than a conversion would. We won't promise specific dimensions or fees before we've studied your actual parcel, so the feasibility check — lot, setbacks, utilities, slope — is always the first step on a Casa de Oro ADU. Then we handle the County permit set, engineering, and build.
