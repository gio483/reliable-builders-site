Spring Valley sits just east of Lemon Grove and south of La Mesa, and it's one of those East County communities where almost no two streets feel the same. You'll drive one block of tidy post-war homes, turn a corner, and find hillside lots stepping up a grade that the original developers clearly fought with. We remodel kitchens, bathrooms, and ADUs across all of it — and because we're based a few minutes up the road in La Mesa, Spring Valley is squarely in our everyday service area, not a job we drive across the county to reach.

The thing to understand about Spring Valley is that it's unincorporated. There's no City of Spring Valley; the whole community falls under the County of San Diego. That changes how a remodel gets permitted, and it's one of the first things we sort out before we ever hand you a number.

## The homes here

Spring Valley's housing stock is genuinely varied. There are mid-century tract homes from the 1950s and 1960s making up a big share of it, older ranch-style houses scattered through the valley, and custom hillside homes on the slopes where the land gets steep. Some of the earliest subdivisions were laid out on straight grids that ignored the hilly terrain entirely, which is part of why you'll find such a mix of lot shapes, grades, and build quality within a single neighborhood.

For a remodel, that variety means one honest truth: **expect the home to tell you its real story once the walls are open.** A 1960s Spring Valley kitchen or bathroom was built to the codes of its day, and we routinely find aging galvanized supply lines, two-prong circuits with no ground, original cast-iron drains, and the occasional unpermitted "improvement" a previous owner added over a weekend. That's not a crisis — it's just what remodeling a home with a few decades on it looks like. It's also exactly why we'd rather give you an honest number that accounts for what's likely behind the drywall than a cheap bid that balloons the first week.

The upside is that these homes often have good bones and larger lots than you'd get in newer construction — room to open up a cramped galley kitchen or add the ADU that so many Spring Valley families are after. It's also why we spend real time walking a Spring Valley home before we quote it: two houses on the same street can need very different work, and the only honest way to price a remodel is to understand the one you actually own.

## Permitting in Spring Valley

Because Spring Valley is unincorporated, permits run through the **County of San Diego's Planning & Development Services** — specifically the Building Services Division, which issues permits for residential work in the unincorporated areas. That's a different office than the one a La Mesa or El Cajon homeowner would use, and the submittal process and plan-check expectations have their own rhythm. We handle all of it: the application, the plan set, the inspections. You never stand in a County line.

A straightforward kitchen or bathroom remodel that stays inside the existing footprint — new cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures, lighting, and like-for-like plumbing and electrical — is a permittable project and usually the simpler track. Where it gets more involved is anything structural or anything that grows the home: taking out a wall to open up a kitchen (we confirm whether it's load-bearing and engineer a beam if it is), relocating plumbing, adding square footage, or building an ADU. Spring Valley's hillside lots can also bring grading or slope considerations that the flatter valley parcels don't. We figure out which track your Spring Valley project falls on before we quote it, so the County permit path is part of the plan from day one.

## ADUs in Spring Valley

ADUs — accessory dwelling units, or granny flats — are one of the most common reasons Spring Valley homeowners call us. Usually it's for an aging parent, an adult kid who can't crack the San Diego rental market, or long-term rental income on a lot that has the space for it. Spring Valley's larger lots make it a genuinely good place for one.

Because Spring Valley is unincorporated, your ADU follows **California's statewide ADU laws as administered by the County of San Diego**. In broad strokes, that framework now lets most single-family lots add a detached ADU, convert an existing garage, or add a junior ADU, within the state's size and setback rules — and recent state updates have made the path easier than it was a decade ago. We're careful not to promise specific dimensions or fees before we've looked at your actual parcel, because the real answer depends on your lot size, setbacks, utilities, and slope. That feasibility check is the first thing we do on any Spring Valley ADU, before anyone falls in love with a floor plan. Then we handle the County permit set, the engineering, and the build.
