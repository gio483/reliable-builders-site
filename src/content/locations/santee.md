## The homes here

Santee started as ranchland and farm plots along the San Diego River, and it stayed rural longer than a lot of the county. The modern city really took shape after the war — housing tracts began replacing the open valley floor through the 1950s and 1960s, and the big wave of growth came in the 1970s and 1980s. The city didn't even incorporate until 1980. So when people call Santee a "newer" suburb, that's only half true: you've got genuinely newer construction near Town Center and the later developments, mixed in with a large stock of homes that are now 40 to 60 years old.

That mix is exactly why a one-size-fits-all remodeler gets Santee wrong. A 1970s single-story tract home has different realities than a home built in the last couple of decades. In the older Santee homes, we routinely find aluminum branch wiring from the era, original cast-iron or early ABS drains, aging water heaters and panels, and bathrooms that were "updated" once in the '90s without a permit. Those aren't dealbreakers — they're just conditions, and good remodeling means pricing for the real condition of the house instead of handing you a cheap number that jumps the moment we open a wall.

The payoff is that these are well-built, family-sized homes on comfortable lots. A Santee kitchen or bath remodel done honestly doesn't chase trends — it fixes what's tired, brings the systems up to today's code, and gives a family a space that works for how they actually live, cook, and get three people out the door on a school morning.

## Permitting in Santee

Santee runs its own show here. Permits for remodeling work go through the **City of Santee's Planning & Building Department**, not the county — so the process is local, and we handle it for you. The city moved everything to an online permitting portal, which means submittals, plan check, fees, and inspection scheduling all run through the city's system. We pull the permits and manage the inspections so you're never the one learning a government portal at 9pm.

A straightforward kitchen or bathroom remodel that stays inside the existing footprint — cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures, lighting, and like-for-like plumbing and electrical — is a permittable project in Santee and usually moves cleanly through plan check. Where it gets more involved is anything structural or anything that adds square footage: taking out a wall to open up a kitchen (we verify whether it's load-bearing and engineer a beam if it is), relocating plumbing, or building an ADU. We figure out which track your Santee project falls on before we quote it, so the permit path is baked into the plan from day one rather than becoming the thing that stalls the job halfway through.

## ADUs in Santee

ADUs — accessory dwelling units, or granny flats — are a big reason Santee families call us. This is a city where multiple generations often want to stay close: aging parents, an adult kid saving up, or a long-term rental that helps carry the mortgage. California's statewide ADU laws made these far easier to build than they used to be, and the City of Santee processes ADU applications under that framework alongside its own zoning, with the state's required timeline for a decision once your application is complete.

In practice, most single-family lots in Santee can support either a detached ADU or a garage conversion, within the state's size and setback rules. Santee's generous lots and the number of homes with detached or oversized garages make it a particularly good place for a garage-conversion ADU, though which option is right depends entirely on your specific parcel, utilities, and setbacks. That's the first thing we check — before anyone falls in love with a floor plan — so you know what's realistic on your actual lot. We handle the feasibility check, the permit set, the engineering, and the build, so the finished Santee ADU is legal, financeable, and genuinely livable.
