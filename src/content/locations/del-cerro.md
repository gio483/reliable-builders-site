Del Cerro — "of the hill" — climbs the slopes between Lake Murray and San Diego State, bordered by Mission Trails Regional Park and known for exactly what the name promises: hillside homes, canyon and city views, and larger lots than most of the surrounding neighborhoods. It's one of the more established and sought-after pockets of eastern San Diego, and it attracts homeowners who intend to stay. When they call us for a kitchen, bathroom, or ADU, the project is almost always about making a home they already love work better for the next 20 years — and taking advantage of a view most people would pay a fortune for.

Those hillside lots are what make Del Cerro beautiful, and they're also what make remodeling here a little different from the flatlands.

## The homes here

Del Cerro grew up mainly through the 1960s, '70s, and into the '80s, as families moved in for the newer homes, the bigger lots, and the schools. The result is a mix: single-story ranch homes, split-levels that step down the grade, and two-story houses, many of them custom and many deliberately oriented to catch the view out toward Lake Murray and the East County mountains. It's a less uniform neighborhood than a post-war tract — you'll see a real range of eras and styles on a single street.

For a remodel, the view-oriented, hillside nature of Del Cerro shapes everything. Split-level and multi-story homes mean plumbing and electrical runs are longer and less direct than in a single-story ranch, which matters when we're relocating a kitchen or adding a bathroom. Homes from this era still carry the usual older-home surprises — aging supply lines, panels sized for a smaller electrical load, drains near the end of their service life — and the ones built into the slope sometimes have retaining walls, drainage, and foundation details worth understanding before any wall comes down. We'd rather flag all of that honestly up front than hand you a cheap number that balloons once we see the real condition of things.

The upside is enormous. Del Cerro kitchens and primary suites that get opened up and turned toward the windows become genuinely special spaces — the kind of remodel where the finished room finally does justice to the lot it sits on.

## Permitting in Del Cerro

Del Cerro is part of the City of San Diego, so permits for remodeling work here go through the **City of San Diego's Development Services Department**. A kitchen or bathroom remodel that stays within the existing footprint — cabinets, counters, tile, fixtures, lighting, and like-for-like plumbing and electrical — is a permittable project, and we pull the permits and schedule the inspections so you don't have to deal with the City yourself.

The hillside geography is where Del Cerro permitting can get more involved than a flat lot. Structural changes — removing a wall to open a kitchen toward the view, adding square footage, or building onto a sloped parcel — can bring in grading, drainage, retaining, and in some cases geotechnical considerations that a flatland project never touches. The City of San Diego's review process is thorough, and slope-related items add steps. We sort out which track a project falls on before we quote it, so the permit path is part of the plan from day one rather than a surprise that stalls the job.

## ADUs in Del Cerro

ADUs — accessory dwelling units, or granny flats — are a frequent reason Del Cerro homeowners reach out, often to keep an aging parent close or give an adult kid a place of their own. California's statewide ADU laws have made these far easier to build than they used to be, and the City of San Diego runs its own program on top of that framework (the local rules sit in San Diego Municipal Code Chapter 14), including a streamlined review track for qualifying ADUs.

Del Cerro's larger lots are often a real advantage here — there's frequently room for a detached unit that a smaller parcel couldn't fit. The slope cuts both ways, though: a well-placed detached ADU can capture the same views the main house enjoys, while a steep or awkward portion of the lot may need thoughtful siting, access, and drainage. That's why the first thing we do is check your specific parcel — grade, setbacks, access, and utilities — before anyone falls for a floor plan. We handle the feasibility review, the permit set, the engineering, and the build, so the finished ADU is legal, financeable, and somewhere you'd actually want a family member to live. Most ADU projects start around **$150K** and move up with size, site work, and finishes.
